## 1. Phone number capture on popups (2:40)

**AJ:** We're launching in the US now. One question that comes straight to mind: phone number on the popup on your website. It's something we've wanted to test for a while. We're trying to build up our conversion rate on the US side, and it's something we're hesitant to test. Is it genuinely just different in the US compared to the UK — where collecting the phone number is so normalized that it doesn't affect you?

**Juan (3:14):** It's pretty common. I've had a couple of clients where we ran tests, and I haven't seen any increase in conversion rate. Of course you probably don't expect to see an increase in conversion rate from adding friction — but I haven't seen a huge decrease either. And at the end of the day, SMS is probably one of the most profitable channels for retention. The most successful brands I've been working with are collecting SMS on the popup.

**AJ (3:44):** Do you have any rough numbers in your head for what you've seen when phone numbers were added to a popup that didn't have it originally? Just so I can get a rough understanding of what the numbers would look like after we've added it.

**Juan:** In terms of conversion rate, or in terms of take rate for popups?

**AJ:** Both would be helpful.

**Juan (4:10):** I haven't seen any significant decrease in conversion rate from adding the SMS/phone ask on the popup. In terms of take rate, I'd say about **60% of the people who start the popup leave the SMS**. So 60–70% should be a good number to assume.

**AJ (4:36):** A moment ago you said it's one of the biggest levers for retention. Could you elaborate?

**Juan:** I'm not a retention expert, but from what I've discussed with friends who are heads of retention at other brands, and at Mars Men, it's usually the channel with the highest response rates.

**AJ (5:06):** Any specific tools or apps you'd recommend for SMS?

**Juan:** That's more of a retention thing — I'm not a huge expert on what to use, to be honest. I know the biggest brands are using **Postscript**.

## 3. Listicles: template vs. awareness level (10:14)

**AJ:** Let's say this page as a whole — this is just one of the landing pages we use. We've recently seen a lot of brands use listicles, and they seem to be working well. How much have you worked with listicles, and are there any big unlocks you can help us with?

**Juan (10:40):** For sure. A **listicle is a template** — a landing page template. You have advertorials, and then things you build that are a combination of a branded landing page. A listicle is pretty MOFU/BOFU since you're introducing the product right at the beginning, which is fine — but you're limiting yourself if people don't know the product yet. It also depends on the brand and on the ads going to that landing page.

Do you play around with different angles? I was looking at your ads library — do you have specific angles or personas that work well on the ad account?

**AJ (11:28):** Yeah, we have a bunch of different angles and personas that work well. But the main question on our side is: how do we get listicles as an unlock for top of funnel? We've seen them perform pretty much the same as our main landing page in shallow testing, and if anything our NVP for those ads ended up a bit lower. Have you seen it scale for top-of-funnel ads? Is it for more unaware audiences, or can it work for all stages?

**Juan (12:08):** Again, the listicle is a template — depending on how you frame it, it can be a top-of-funnel or mid-funnel page.

- **Top of funnel:** "10 signs that your collagen is way lower than it should be."
- **Mid/bottom of funnel:** "10 reasons why [celebrity] chose this product instead of the generic one."

So I see it as different axes. You have the listicle (the template), and then you play with the **stage of awareness** of the people landing on it.

**The important thing:** if you want to unlock incremental spend, create **specific listicles for different angles**. Usually you have a general listicle — "10 reasons why [brand]'s collagen helps whatever" — and you talk about hair, nails, skin, joints, all of it. You're not focusing on a specific angle. That's a good start.

But once that's working, to expand your TAM and create incremental spend, choose specific angles. "10 reasons why this collagen product improves skin" — focus on one angle, five to seven reasons about that specific angle, then introduce the product. Grab the best stats that speak about skin, duplicate the ads, and point them at that specific landing page. That's more of a head-of-growth decision, but that's the play.

## 4. Duplicating ads onto new landing pages (14:28)

**AJ:** So you're saying we can unlock incremental spend on ads that are already working by duplicating the ads and attaching a hyper-specific landing page?

**Juan (14:35):** Exactly. From what I see across different ad accounts: when you're testing something on Meta, **you're not just testing the ad — you're testing the ad *and* the landing page**. An ad could do normally on a PDP, but if you put it on a quiz or a listicle you can create incremental spend, because at the end of the day you're testing the funnel, not just the ad. The ad is the front end, the first step of the funnel, but there's much more behind it.

**AJ (15:10):** This is actually quite funny — I was talking to Brad recently, he hopped on a call with us, and he said exactly the same thing, that Mars Men were doing exactly what you're describing.

**Juan:** Oh — the guy who has a podcast with Zach?

**AJ:** Yes. So we set this up on Monday night. We went down the logic of duplicating the ad *within the ad set* and attaching the new specific listicle. What we've seen is that **the duplicates ended up taking pretty much all of the spend**, when we assumed it would be somewhat split. That proves incrementality in a sense, because you can increase the budget to allow for more spend — but when it takes all the spend it becomes a question of which one's better, which is the opposite of incrementality. Have you seen that? How do you fix it? Would you ever turn the ad off and push it live in a new ad set? What's your troubleshooting process?

**Juan (16:30):** It all depends on how you've built the ad account. If you have one CBO and you duplicate the ads into a new ad set with a new landing page, and that takes all the spend — that's not incremental. But the question then is: **can I increase the budget?** That would be incremental revenue.

Another way: if you have an ABO campaign or a creative-testing setup, **frame the landing page test as a creative test**. Again, you're testing funnels, not specific ads. Create a new ad set there, give it its own budget, and duplicate the ads to the new landing page. That way you'll see instantly whether it's incremental.

And to answer your question — yes, I've seen it many times that the same ads in different ad sets create way more incremental spend. At Mars Men I can't give you specifics, but we've been able to **double our ad spend in the last six months**, and we're already spending pretty high. I wouldn't say it's only because of these new landing pages — there's way more creative being made too — but landing pages are one of the biggest parts of the formula.

## 5. How many landing pages to test per creative (18:07)

**AJ:** So moving forward, when you have new content and new creators being launched, are you launching them into multiple ad sets with multiple landing pages? What does the structure look like when testing landing pages alongside creatives? I imagine the volume would be insane — a few versions per hook, each hook with five landing pages. How many landing pages would you test? And is it on all new creatives, or only winning creatives?

**Juan (18:49):** Great question. There are two ways to test landing pages:

**1. Angle already winning on the ad account.** Create specific landing pages for it and duplicate the ads in, to unlock more incremental spend. That's what we've been discussing.

**2. A completely new angle.** This needs coherence and cross-functionality across the organization. Say we want to go after women over 80 — you build specific ads that speak to a specific landing page. What I like to do: pick an angle we haven't tested, create **one listicle, one quiz, and one advertorial**, and produce specific creative for those pages. Ideally you also gather new partners, partnerships, or celebrities to actually test the angle and unlock more incremental spend.

So, in summary: (1) an angle that's winning → create a new landing page and duplicate the ads; (2) a new angle you want to test → create specific ads for that specific landing page.

And if you're testing creative, test it against your best landing page or a PDP. If you want to get courageous you can build a specific landing page for that angle if you don't have one. But as you said, it doesn't make sense to test ten different landing pages for one creative. You need to use intuition — pick one, two, or three landing pages you think will work better and test that way.

**AJ (20:45):** Just to clarify — at Mars Men, for every new concept you have high confidence in, you have three different landing pages being tested at the same time when you launch it?

**Juan:** Correct.

## 6. When the duplicate eats all the budget and NVP drops (21:16)

**AJ:** Going back to my earlier question — in a case where it's ABO and the budget is entirely taken by the duplicate ads, what would you advise as the exact next steps? To clarify: it's a bit less efficient. NVP has gone down, so it's less top of funnel. We took that as a loss internally.

**Juan (21:56):** You're trading budget, right. You can play around with the ad account — create a CBO and see if you can start winning there. Another option is to **create more top-of-funnel landing pages and ads**.

You need to really think about **ad-to-landing-page coherence**. If you have bottom-of-funnel ads and you send them to an advertorial because it's a top-of-funnel landing page, there's no congruence — it doesn't make sense, and you'll see a really bad conversion rate because people are already warm.

**AJ (22:37):** Sorry, could you go back a second? You said not to send top-of-funnel ad traffic to a top-of-funnel advertorial?

**Juan (22:43):** Sorry — **bottom-of-funnel ads to top-of-funnel landing pages** doesn't make sense. It's a journey.

What really creates incremental spend on top-of-funnel landing pages: **don't talk about the product in the ad — sell the advertorial, sell the quiz, sell the listicle.** If you introduce the product too fast, people already know about it, and the job of warming up the lead ends up falling on the ad. Then when you send them to the advertorial there's no coherence between ad and landing page.

So if you're seeing ROAS decreasing and new-visitor percentage decreasing, the answer is to start creating more top-of-funnel ads and sending them to top-of-funnel landing pages.

**AJ (23:43):** That makes complete sense. We had one instance where an ad had relatively high NVP, and when we duplicated it with the listicle it dropped the whole thing down and it's now performing as bottom of funnel. I assume it's because of the format of the ad, which we were hesitant about internally before launching.

## 7. What makes a listicle top-of-funnel vs. bottom-of-funnel (24:00)

**AJ:** What's the biggest differentiator between a top-of-funnel listicle and a bottom-of-funnel listicle? What would you separate to make sure a listicle you're making for a confident top-of-funnel ad stays top of funnel for NVP?

**Juan (24:24):** I'm not sure about the Meta crawler, but I am sure about customer psychology. If you're talking about the product from the beginning, people aren't ready to start hearing about the product yet. Collagen is a little saturated in terms of people knowing it and competitors — nothing new under the sun. You probably have a unique mechanism that differentiates you.

So it really depends mostly on **the hook**. 80% of people who land on the page leave. What determines whether a listicle is top or bottom of funnel is basically **the first three reasons and the H1**:

- "This is the real reason why your nails are cracking" → **top of funnel**
- Leading with the product → **middle/bottom of funnel**

## 8. Quizzes (25:23)

**AJ:** I remember checking out a few of the listicles Mars Men used, and a big percentage of them had the product at the top. So do you guys do top-of-funnel listicles?

**Juan (25:39):** Sometimes. What really works for us is **quizzes** — that's one of the best for top of funnel. It's a self-diagnosing tool, and you're not introducing the product from the beginning.

**AJ:** What platform do you use to build the quiz?

**Juan:** We build it internally. We used **Aflow** — I was not a huge fan, it's pretty clunky and annoying to use.

**AJ:** I spent a fair share of time in Aflow. It was a nightmare.

**Juan (26:18):** To be honest, there's nothing there you couldn't build yourself in a couple of days.

**AJ (26:25):** Are you linking the quiz to a PDP with the product at the top, or what type of page does it end at?

**Juan (26:35):** It depends on the ad — you can see it in the ads library, no secret. We're usually taking them to **the PDP**, because the quiz does a fairly thorough job of diagnosing people, explaining that testosterone is a real problem, and why Mars Men is different from other T-boosters out there. **The quiz does all the heavy lifting, and the PDP just completes the purchase.**

**AJ (27:00):** So when you're launching a batch of concepts you're confident are top of funnel, high NVP — if you test three landing pages, is it a PDP and a quiz? Or would you throw in a listicle you made top of funnel? How do you prioritize which landing pages get tested for high-confidence top-of-funnel concepts?

**Juan (27:27):** We have the benefit of spending a lot, so testing a new landing page isn't big in the overall scheme — if it doesn't work it's 1–2% of total spend.

What I'd recommend: the **PDP** (or whichever lander is working best for you), plus a **quiz**. A general quiz usually does really well if you're not speaking to a specific angle. But if the ads speak to a specific angle or persona, **tailoring the quiz to that persona/angle works really, really well**. And you don't need a lot of ads to carry the landing page — five really good ads that are coherent with the quiz or listicle work well.

From what I've seen, **quizzes and listicles are the lowest lift and highest impact. Advertorials are a little harder to crack.**

**AJ (28:37):** Have you ever seen a quiz work as the landing page for a bottom-of-funnel ad?

**Juan:** Yep. It's worked well.

**AJ:** What's the logic behind that?

**Juan (28:54):** People are interested. I've seen some people rush the quiz. My guess is they want to self-diagnose anyway — they know Mars Men, they know the problem, but they want to know what *their* specific problem is. That's off the top of my head. If I had to give you a specific reason with confidence, I couldn't. It's working; I'm not really sure why.

## 9. Measuring incrementality (29:36)

**Founder:** For you guys, is the point of the quizzes and listicles mainly to unlock more incremental spend? And how are you actually measuring whether the spend is incremental — how do you know you couldn't have unlocked that spend anyway?

**Juan (29:55):** Hard to tell with confidence. But **if we're able to scale more budget at the same efficiency, that's a win.** It's hard to attribute whether it's because of new landers or new creatives.

What I've seen: when we do specific ABOs or CBOs and a lander is working really well, it scales while the other campaigns or ad sets keep spending as they used to. That gives me a hint there's incremental spend there.

You're launching so much — promotions, new angles, new ads, new landing pages — so it's hard to say specifically what it is. But what I can tell you is: we created new campaigns with the same ads and a different landing page, the other campaigns maintained the same efficiency at the same spend, and this one increased spend by **15–20%**.

**Founder (31:02):** When you're looking at whether spend is incremental, do you look at the NVP of the original campaign, see if it's changed, and compare it to the NVP of the new one? Is NVP a huge part of the calculation?

**Juan (31:44):** It's part of the equation. Again, it's really difficult to infer causality when you're changing a lot of things at the same time. But at the end of the day what we're really looking at is: **are we able to increase spend at the efficiency goal?** That's about it.

## 10. Trading CAC for volume (32:04)

**Founder:** Let's say you can scale an extra 20% with the new landing page, but CAC also rises 10–20%. Is that still a win for you? Are you just looking for more spend in general, or do you always work at a set max?

**Juan (32:25):** That's a business question. We like to keep CAC constant. So for us, increasing spend 20% and increasing CAC 15% is definitely not a win.

**Founder:** But what if it was a 5% increase in CAC with much higher volume?

**Juan (32:45):** Again, hard to infer causality from correlation — I'm not sure that wouldn't have happened anyway without the landing page. But **if it were my business, I'd be happy spending 20–30% more with a 5% increase in CAC, especially if I have LTV.** If I'm selling helmets, I'm a little bit screwed. But if you're selling consumable products, a 5% increase is something you recoup in two more weeks or a month. Is it a monthly subscription?

**Founder:** Yeah. Got it.

## Quick reference — Juan's key points

| Topic | Takeaway |
|---|---|
| SMS on popup | No meaningful CVR decrease; ~60–70% of popup starters give their phone. Worth it for retention. Postscript is what big brands use. |
| Subscription offer | Push toward 80–85% take rate. Test hiding one-time purchase or demoting it to a hyperlinked line below the CTA. Make delivery frequency explicit when it varies by quantity. |
| Listicles | A template, not a funnel stage. The H1 and first 3 reasons determine awareness level. Angle-specific listicles unlock incremental spend; general ones are the starting point. |
| Testing landing pages | You're testing the funnel, not the ad. Duplicate winning ads into a new ad set with its own budget + a new lander to see incrementality instantly. |
| Ad ↔ lander coherence | Top-of-funnel ads should sell the quiz/listicle/advertorial, not the product. Bottom-of-funnel ads into top-of-funnel landers kills CVR. |
| Landing page mix | Per high-confidence concept: ~3 landers (best PDP/lander + quiz, angle-tailored where possible). ~5 coherent ads is enough to carry a lander. |
| Format ranking | Quizzes and listicles = lowest lift, highest impact. Advertorials are harder to crack. |
| Incrementality test | Can you scale budget at the same efficiency while other campaigns hold their spend and efficiency? |
| CAC trade-off | +20–30% spend for +5% CAC is acceptable on consumables with LTV. +20% spend for +15% CAC is not. |
