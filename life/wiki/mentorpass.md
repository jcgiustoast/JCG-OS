## Rules & constraints

### The sharp risk: §5.6 confidentiality (not §5.2)
Mentorship is literally *"tell me what's working at your brand."* It is **synchronous, unscripted, and the member is paying precisely to extract specifics** — a far leakier surface than a written LinkedIn post Juan can edit before publishing.

Per [[professional]], the **Mars Men playbook itself is Confidential Information** under **§5.6, which is geography-blind and runs Term + 2 years**. So the operative rule is not "don't name Mars Men" — it's **don't hand over the MM playbook**, even anonymized, even to a non-competitor.

**Working rule:** teach the *method* (how to structure a test, how to think about subscription LTV, how to read a CRO result), never the *proprietary specifics* (MM's actual test results, funnel math, LP performance, retention numbers, roadmap). Method is portable and it's what mentees are paying for anyway. Specifics are the contract breach.

#### It has now been crossed once (2026-08-26)

Three disclosures on the second call:

- MM's **ad spend trajectory** — how much it had grown and over what period. Prefaced with "at Mars Men I can't give you specifics," then followed by the specific in the same sentence.
- The **result of a specific MM landing-page test** — the spend increase a new lander produced while other campaigns held efficiency.
- MM's **testing cadence** — how many landing pages run against each new high-confidence concept. Volunteered on request and confirmed.

One thing that looked like a disclosure was not: the quiz-to-product-page routing is visible in the public ads library, and Juan said so on the call. That call was correct.

These map exactly onto the three categories the revised Phase 1 firewall prohibits ([[content-strategy]], 2026-08-27): *never the number, the test result, or the implementation*.

**The sequence matters.** The call was 2026-08-26 and the rule was codified 2026-08-27 — the next day. So nothing written was breached at the time, and it is possible the call itself helped prompt the revision. What it does is expose the gap the rule leaves open: the firewall was written for **published content**, and a post can be edited before it ships. A call cannot. The same three prohibitions need a spoken-word version, which is what the substitutes below are.

**The mechanic worth naming:** the specific is what makes the answer good. The mentee is paying for the number, so every honest, generous answer pulls toward the thing the contract blocks. This is not a discipline failure, it's the shape of the format, and it will recur on every call unless there's a prepared substitute.

**Prepared substitute — say the shape, withhold the magnitude.** "We ran the same ads against a new lander in its own campaign and the existing campaigns held their spend and efficiency" carries the entire teaching value. The percentage adds nothing the mentee can act on and is the only part that breaches. Same for cadence: "more than one, hand-picked, not a matrix" teaches the principle; the number is MM's operating procedure.

### §5.2 — who is allowed to book him
**§5.2 bars any activity "competitive with or adverse to" Mars Men, WORLDWIDE, during the term.** On a marketplace, Juan does not choose who books — bookings come to him. That inverts the usual vetting.

**Decline rule:** if the booker is a **men's hormone / testosterone / male-wellness-supplement brand**, decline and refund. This is the **Jello** line ([[projects]] → Declined/blocked) — product/demographic overlap is the real risk axis, not geography. Everything else (nail care, helmets, teen grooming, non-hormone supplements) is clean under any reasonable read.
- **Action needed:** confirm MentorPass lets a mentor decline/cancel a booking without penalty. If it does not, that is a structural problem with being listed at all.

### Content constraint carries over — and it moved
Juan cannot discuss Meta Ads / performance marketing **as advice** ([[professional]], narrowed 2026-08-27 in [[content-strategy]]) — not his lane at MM and it would read strangely to Zach/Benjamin. There is now a carve-out: how CRO experiments affect paid media, framed as measurement rather than media buying, is fine. Sellable topics remain the ones he actually owns: **CRO & experimentation, subscription economics, retention/LTV, eComm finance, LP design/dev.**

**The second call sat right on that line.** Roughly half of it was budget-pool structure, where to place a duplicated ad, and diagnosing a drop in new-visitor share — and Juan handled all of it fluently. Two observations follow:

- The constraint is **political, not about competence.** He plainly knows this material. The reason to stay quiet is how it reads to Zach and Benjamin, not whether he can answer.
- There is a **defensible public version**: the landing-page half of paid acquisition. Ad-to-page coherence, funnel testing, awareness matching. All of it is CRO in substance, all of it is his, and it is where the good material from this call lives. The account mechanics — CBO versus ABO, where the duplicate goes — stay private. See the firewall split in [[landing-page-testing]].

A private call does not breach the *public*-discussion rule. But MentorPass reviews and profiles are public, so if a mentee reviews him for paid-media help, the distinction stops holding. Worth watching.

### Firewall — the direction that matters
The usual rule is "never name Mars Men in an ASTEROI context." **MentorPass runs the other way.** It's a personal-brand surface, so leading with the MM credential is consistent with his LinkedIn and fine.

The risk is the **reverse**: the profile is public and indexed, and a Mars Men colleague could find it. So **the profile must not position Juan as an agency founder** — no "Founder, ASTEROI," no agency services, no client roster. MentorPass is the one place where "MM Head of eComm" and "agency owner" would appear in the same frame. Mentor = thought leader (safe). Mentor + agency = the firewall breach.

### Routing converted leads
A mentee who wants ongoing work must route to **Tincho / ASTEROI**, never to Juan personally — he has no capacity.
- **Verify first:** most marketplaces bar taking a relationship **off-platform**. Check MentorPass's terms before converting any mentee into an ASTEROI retainer. If circumvention is barred, MentorPass is a **brand/credibility channel that happens to pay**, not a client-acquisition funnel — which materially changes how much it's worth investing in.

## What two sessions have settled

**Track B verdict, provisional: this is credibility that pays, not a pipeline.** The stated test was whether any mentee turns out to be an ASTEROI-shaped lead. Two sessions, two no's, and they failed in different directions — one wanted a job, one wanted knowledge for an in-house team. That's weak evidence but it points one way, and it matches the structural read: people who pay for an hour of advice are, by revealed preference, people who intend to do the work themselves. Off-platform conversion terms are still unchecked, but they now matter less than they did — there has been nothing to convert.

**Implication for time.** If this is a credibility asset rather than an acquisition channel, cap the investment accordingly. Take the calls, bank the reviews and the material, don't build a funnel behind it.

**The unexpected return: it produces content.** Both sessions generated a topic page the vault didn't have ([[subscription-cro-experimentation]], [[landing-page-testing]]), and the second surfaced a concrete spec change for a live tool — the LP Generator builds general listicles, while Juan's own answer on the call was that angle-specific pages are where incremental spend comes from, and that the headline plus first three items set the awareness stage. Being interrogated by a sharp head of growth is the forcing function; he articulated frameworks on that call that he had never written down. That may be the real yield of this channel.

## Watch
- Whether the §5.2 decline rule ever gets triggered in practice. Two bookings in, both skincare — it hasn't come up yet.
- Whether the confidentiality slip repeats now that it's named. The prepared substitutes above are the fix; the test is whether he uses them under live pressure.
- Whether a mentee ever reviews him publicly for paid-media advice, which would collapse the private/public distinction the content constraint rests on.
- Whether session prep starts consuming meaningful hours. Capacity-safe is the premise; two sessions in it still holds.
