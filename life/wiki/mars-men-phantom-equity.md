# Mars Men Phantom Equity Plan — Analysis & Decision Record

## Status
**SIGNED (2026-08-01) — as-is, no modifications.** Juan signed the Award Agreement under Mars Health's **2026 Phantom Equity Plan** (11,175 phantom shares, $0 strike). It **replaces** the contractual **0.1%-on-sale** (per Juan); the Award's supersession clause extinguishes the old right on signing. Before signing Juan asked for three changes — acceleration on a sale, a without-cause carve-out on the 6-month tail, and anti-dilution. **Mars Men declined all three** (to keep the plan consistent across recipients), offering only non-binding "may consider case-by-case waivers" language on the first two. Juan signed anyway — consistent with the standing decision that MM is the **ASTEROI bridge** (salary + credential + cover), not an equity play. He logged a real let-down first (he'd walked from PAK's better equity terms), then reframed to the bridge logic: the equity was never why he stayed, and those reasons (nearer/surer exit, low disruption, credential, protected ASTEROI bandwidth) are all intact.

### Mars Men's response, decoded (declined — 2026-07-31)
- **Q1 acceleration:** "Plan doesn't provide automatic acceleration; company *may consider* case-by-case." = restating existing Board discretion, no binding change. Worth ~nothing.
- **Q2 without-cause protection:** "not revising this provision… *may consider* permitted modifications or waivers based on circumstances." = declined the carve-out; non-binding goodwill only. The one winnable ask, not won.
- **Q3 dilution:** "your number of Phantom Shares is fixed and won't decrease… but may represent a smaller percentage of fully-diluted cap." = confirms the dilution exactly as analyzed; the fixed *count* was never the risk, the *percentage* dilutes. Comfort-spin, not protection.

## What it is
Phantom equity = a **contingent cash bonus dressed as equity.** No shares, no ownership, no voting, no dividends. It pays a cash amount **only if Mars Health is sold** (a "Change in Control"). If the company never sells, it is worth **$0**.

## The grant (from the Award Agreement)
- **Participant:** ASTEROI LLC (a WY LLC — 100% Juan). Signed for MM by **Benjamin Smith, CEO**.
- **Phantom shares:** **11,175**
- **Strike price:** **$0**
- **Grant date:** July 20, 2026
- **Vesting Commencement Date (VCD):** **October 20, 2025** (backdated ~9 months — favorable)
- **Total plan pool:** 558,773 phantom shares. Juan's grant = exactly **2.0% of the pool.**
- **Governing law:** Delaware (Juan's MM contract is Wisconsin — different jurisdiction).

## What 11,175 is worth (load-bearing inference)
The share count was sized off the old **0.1%** (Juan confirmed). Math is consistent: 11,175 ÷ 558,773 = 2.0% of the pool; if the pool is 5.0% of fully-diluted, Juan's slice = **~0.1% of fully-diluted**, implying a fully-diluted count near ~11.175M. **CONFIRM against the actual cap table** — the whole model scales linearly with this fraction.

Crucial: it's **0.1% of Net Proceeds, not 0.1% of the headline price.** Net Proceeds = sale price − fees − the **full preferred liquidation stack** (original issue price + accrued Series A dividends + a **5% investor top-up**). Phantom sits **behind** preferred, like common stock.

## The five gates (all must be true, or $0)
1. A qualifying **sale** happens (>50% third-party acquisition, or substantially-all-assets sale).
2. It **closes on/after Oct 20, 2026** (1-year cliff).
3. **Net Proceeds exceed the preferred stack** (subordination).
4. **ASTEROI is still engaged 6 months AFTER closing** — the killer (see below).
5. ASTEROI signs a **broad general release** within 60 days of closing.

## Vesting
- **Cliff, then linear.** Nothing vests if the sale closes before Oct 20, 2026. The moment that date passes, it jumps to **33%** (retroactive credit for the 12 months since VCD), then **+1/36 per month to 100% at Oct 20, 2028.**
- Effective % = 0.1% × (full months since Oct 20 2025 ÷ 36), capped at 100%, zero before Oct 2026.
- Vesting **locks at closing**; the extra 6 months of service only *unlocks payment* of what vested at close — it doesn't add vesting.
- Cliff is **nearly behind Juan** — only bites if a sale closes before ~Oct 2026.

## The sharpest risk — the 6-month post-close service tail
Must remain in Continuous Service **through the 6-month anniversary of closing.** If service ends **for ANY reason** before then — *including the acquirer terminating or not renewing ASTEROI without cause* — **all shares, vested and unvested, forfeit for $0.**
- Real-world trigger is **not malice, it's redundancy** — an acquirer with its own eCom team cuts the role, and the tail wipes the payout as a side effect.
- Also a golden handcuff in reverse: "for any reason" includes *Juan* leaving, so a bad post-acquisition environment traps him into grinding 6 months to collect.
- Note on who funds it: a payout like this is almost always **carved from deal proceeds, not the buyer's pocket** — so a buyer is usually economically *neutral* to whether Juan gets paid (his ~0.1% is rounding-error on a large deal). The danger is redundancy, not a buyer surgically dodging the bill.

## Other structural risks
- **IPO likely does NOT trigger** (no single >50% buyer, no asset sale). If MM's path is public markets, this can end at $0.
- **Only the FIRST qualifying event counts** — a modest majority-recap "uses up" the one trigger; a bigger later exit pays nothing extra. An *existing* investor buying to >50% is carved out as a non-event.
- **Board (Administrator) has sole discretion** on Net Proceeds, the denominator, and non-cash valuation; interpretations are "final, binding, conclusive," no de novo review, upheld absent "manifest fraud."
- **Denominator dilutes over time** — measured at closing, it grows with every future raise/option/phantom grant; Juan's fixed 11,175 only shrinks as a %, with no anti-dilution. Future rounds hit twice: bigger denominator **and** a taller preferred stack ahead of him.
- **Tax:** ordinary income, withheld, 409A; paid to a WY LLC while Juan is a **Spanish tax resident** → cross-border complexity. Get an advisor before any payout.

## The replacement problem (why signing isn't free)
It **replaces**, not adds to, the contractual 0.1%. The Award Agreement's **"Entire Agreement / supersedes prior agreements" clause** means **the act of signing likely extinguishes the contractual 0.1%.** Trade, axis by axis:

| | Old (contractual 0.1%) | New (phantom) |
|---|---|---|
| Forfeiture | Paid if there at the sale (likely) | Forfeit for ANY reason unless engaged 6mo post-close |
| Cliff | Likely none | $0 if sold before Oct 2026 |
| Base of % | Possibly gross | Net Proceeds (after preferred + 5% top-up) |
| Enforceability | Contract right (WI law) | Board sole discretion |
| Condition | None | Broad release within 60 days |

Certain downgrades regardless of old wording: the **6-month tail**, the **cliff**, **Board discretion**, the **release**. Unknown until the old clause is pulled: whether the old base was **gross vs. net** (potentially the biggest swing).

**Leverage reframe (matters given Juan feels cornered):** they can withdraw the *phantom offer* freely, but they **cannot unilaterally erase a contractual 0.1%** — only Juan's signature does that. So *doing nothing keeps the better right* (if the 0.1% is a signed, standing term and his engagement isn't tied to signing). The worst case of "not signing" is often the status quo, not zero. Two facts to verify: (a) is the 0.1% a signed, in-force contract term? (b) is anyone making signing a condition of staying?

## The one ask worth making (if any)
Not a "full ramp on sale" (single-trigger) — that only helps in the scenario where Juan's already fine (kept through the transition) and does **nothing** in the scenarios that zero him (getting cut), because the tail forfeits even accelerated shares. Instead, one line that protects the zero *and* folds in acceleration:

> "If a Change in Control occurs and the acquirer terminates or doesn't renew ASTEROI's engagement without cause before the 6-month anniversary, my shares vest in full and remain payable."

Frames as **fairness, not greed** → easiest to get a yes. It's the same instinct as a full ramp, aimed at the risk that can actually hurt him. Even from a no-leverage position, this is the single ask to spend any capital on — as a neutral, low-friction request, not a fight.

## Value model (at a $1B acquisition)
Assumes ~0.1% slice, fully vested (sale on/after Oct 2028):

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Gross sale price | $1,000M |
| − fees (~2.5%) + preferred stack + 5% top-up | −$100M to $220M |
| = Net Proceeds | ~$780M–$900M |
| × ~0.1% | **~$780K–$900K gross** |
| − tax (ordinary income, ~45–50%, cross-border TBD) | ~half |
| **≈ in pocket** | **~$450K–$500K** |

- **At $1B, subordination barely matters** — the preferred stack is noise against $1B. Subordination is a *small-exit* problem; it guts modest exits (or zeros a sale below the preference stack) but not this one.
- **Scales with timing (vesting):** ~33% (~$285K) if sold Oct 2026 → ~67% (~$570K) Oct 2027 → 100% (~$850K) Oct 2028+.
- Everything scales **linearly** with the true fraction — if the cap table says 0.15% or 0.05%, adjust ±50%.

## Exit odds — the Gruns fact-check (2026-07-27)
Juan's bull case rested on: *"Mars Men has the same investor (L Catterton) as Gruns, which sold fast."* **Verified via web search — the premise is false.**
- **TRUE:** Gruns sold to **Unilever for ~$1.2B**, closed **June 1, 2026**, ~30 months after founding (Aug 2023); hit $300M+ revenue in ~2 years. A real, huge, fast exit.
- **FALSE:** **Gruns was NOT an L Catterton company.** Series B ($35M, $500M valuation, Apr 2025) was **led by Headline**; other investors Sugar Capital, Selva Ventures, Able Partners, Plus Capital, GRT SHT Ventures, and Unilever (strategic). No L Catterton in any source.
- **Lesson:** the fast exit was driven by **explosive sustained growth + a hot category + a strategic buyer (Unilever)** — not the financial sponsor. The right comp axis for Mars Men is **growth trajectory, not shared investor.** On growth (0→$100M run rate in <18 months), MM genuinely rhymes with Gruns — so a fast exit isn't fantasy, but the bull case rests on **MM sustaining growth + a strategic buyer appearing**, which nobody can predict or control.
- **Base rate:** most PE-backed consumer brands that exit at all take **5–8+ years** from investment; fast exits are the exception.

## Decision synthesis
1. **The phantom is a free lottery ticket, not a reason to stay.** For it to pay Juan, a sale must land in his ≤2-year window, after Oct 2026, above the preference stack, with him retained 6 months post-close — a low-probability compound bet. **Don't anchor the stay/leave call on it.**
2. **Given Juan's ≤2-year horizon**, his only realistic collection path is an **early sale while still engaged → partial vest.** That makes the double-trigger carve-out (above) the *relevant* protection, and confirms not to over-weight the phantom.
3. **Stay/leave decides on the [[strategy]] gates, not exit odds:** $25K+/mo recurring ASTEROI with clients Tincho can service; a live inbound engine; 3+ months runway. Currently ~half of gate 1, gate 2 dormant, constraint = demand. Plan exits ~Q2 2027; ≤2yr is the backstop. **Don't leave early off-plan** — the MM salary funds the runway and the credential powers the inbound while he's still in the seat.
4. **Don't let the phantom downgrade sour the stay decision emotionally** — it removes a reason to stay *for upside*, but Juan was never staying for upside (salary + credential + learning, all intact).
